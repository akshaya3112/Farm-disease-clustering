The Distance Metric Dilemma: Euclidean K-Means vs Manhattan K-Medians

Geometric Clarification

Euclidean K-Means (L2 Norm)

Standard K-Means optimizes the sum of squared Euclidean distances:

Objective = ∑ ||x_i - μ_c||²
Optimal Centroid = Mean(Χ_c)

Because continuous vitals (Age, Temperature) were standardized to unit variance, Euclidean distance naturally incorporates smooth continuous variance along those axes.

Manhattan K-Medians (L1 Norm)

Standard scikit-learn does not support Manhattan distance K-Means because taking the mean does not minimize L1 distance! Under L1 norm:

Objective = ∑ ∑ |x_{i,j} - m_{c,j}|
Optimal Centroid = Coordinate-wise Median(Χ_c)

We implemented a fully vectorized K-Medians algorithm. On binary symptom features, Manhattan distance is identical to Hamming distance (counting differing symptom flags), preventing squared distortion of sparse binary vectors.
