Profile 1 (24.1% of Herd)

Senior Cattle Appetite Loss Syndrome

🐂

Common in older dairy cattle and buffalo (avg 11.9 yrs). Characterized by severe loss of appetite (75.7%) and lethargy/depression (51.2%) without high acute fever.

💡 Care Advice: Offer palatable soft green feeds, ensure adequate hydration, and monitor rumination.
Profile 2 (15.9% of Herd)

Acute Respiratory-Diaphoretic Crisis

🚨

Severe respiratory distress marked by heavy sweating, acute fatigue, and shortness of breath (~51% each). Historical cross-check shows 100% correlation with Anthrax cases.

🚨 Emergency Advice: Immediate isolation. High suspicion of acute infectious illness. Do not slaughter; call vet immediately.
Profile 3 (32.2% of Herd)

Young Stock Sub-Febrile & Limb Pattern

🐐

Dominant in young goats and sheep (avg 4.8 yrs) with lower/normal temperature (~101.2°F). Presents with focal lumps (42.1%) and lameness (22.9%).

💡 Care Advice: Inspect hooves and skin nodules. Check for flock lameness and vector (tick/fly) exposure.
Profile 4 (27.8% of Herd)

Acute High-Fever Systemic Illness

🔥

Marked by severe hyperthermia (mean temp 103.8°F) across all livestock species, accompanied by acute appetite loss (78.7%) and depression.

⚠️ Care Advice: Cool animal with wet cloths in shade. Provide electrolyte water and administer prescribed antipyretics.
