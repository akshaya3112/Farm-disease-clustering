Why Label Encoding is Flawed for Distance-Based Clustering

Geometric Foundation

The Label Encoding Trap

Label encoding maps nominal classes to ordinal integers (e.g., Buffalo=0, Cow=1, Goat=2, Sheep=3). When fed to K-Means or K-Medians, the algorithm computes Euclidean distance:

dist(Sheep, Buffalo) = (3 - 0)² = 9
dist(Cow, Buffalo) = (1 - 0)² = 1

This artificially forces the model to believe Sheep is 3 times more dissimilar from Buffalo than Cow is, corrupting cluster boundaries with false metric topology.

Our Multi-Hot / One-Hot Solution

We mapped the nominal species into orthogonal basis vectors (4 binary indicators) and restructured the three symptom slots into a unified 24-dimensional binary multi-hot feature space.

dist_Hamming(Sheep, Buffalo) = 1.0 (equidistant)
Multi-hot symptom vector: x ∈ {0, 1}²⁴

This guarantees every livestock species is equidistant in space, and symptom presence is recorded neutrally regardless of whether it appeared in Symptom 1, 2, or 3.
